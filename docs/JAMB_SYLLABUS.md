# JAMB UTME Syllabus — Subject Outline

Reference outline for the 10 core UTME subjects covered in this app. Each subject
has a 200-question bank in `assets/questions/<id>.json`, drawn from these official
syllabus topics. Use of English is compulsory for all candidates; candidates then
pick three other subjects relevant to their intended course.

## 1. Use of English (id: `eng`)
**Section A — Comprehension & Summary**
- Comprehension passages (description, narration, exposition, argumentation/persuasion)
- Summary writing (condensing passages to core ideas)

**Section B — Lexis & Structure**
- Sentence interpretation, word & phrase meaning, idioms
- Grammar: parts of speech, subject–verb agreement, tense, concord
- Sentence types (simple, compound, complex), punctuation, spelling
- Antonyms, synonyms, contextual vocabulary

**Section C — Oral Forms**
- Vowels and consonants, phonetics/phonology
- Stress (word & sentence), intonation, rhyme/rhythm
- The prescribed JAMB prose text (e.g. *The Life Changer* / *The Lekki Headmaster*)

## 2. Mathematics (id: `mat`)
Number bases; fractions, decimals, approximations & percentages; indices, logarithms
& surds; sets; polynomials; variation; inequalities; arithmetic & geometric
progressions; binary operations; matrices & determinants; Euclidean geometry;
mensuration; loci; coordinate geometry (straight lines, circles); trigonometry;
differentiation; applications of differentiation; integration; representation of
data (statistics); measures of location & dispersion; probability; permutations &
combinations.

## 3. Physics (id: `phy`)
Measurements & units; scalars & vectors; motion; Newton's laws; friction; work,
energy & power; momentum & impulse; gravitation; equilibrium of forces; heat &
temperature; thermal expansion; gas laws; quantity of heat & calorimetry; waves;
sound; light (reflection, refraction, lenses, mirrors); optical instruments;
electrostatics; capacitors; current electricity; magnetism; electromagnetic
induction; alternating current; modern physics (atomic structure, radioactivity,
photoelectric effect).

## 4. Chemistry (id: `che`)
Separation of mixtures & purification; chemical combination (laws, moles); kinetic
theory of matter & gas laws; atomic structure & bonding; air; water; solubility;
environmental pollution; acids, bases & salts; oxidation–reduction (redox);
electrolysis; energy changes (thermochemistry); rates of chemical reaction; chemical
equilibrium; non-metals & their compounds; metals & their compounds; organic
compounds (hydrocarbons, functional groups); chemistry & industry.

## 5. Biology (id: `bio`)
**Section A — Variety of Organisms**: characteristics & classification of living
things, viruses.
**Section B — Form & Functions**: cell structure & organisation; nutrition (plant &
animal); transport; respiration; excretion; support & movement; reproduction;
growth; co-ordination & control (nervous & hormonal).
**Section C — Ecology**: factors affecting distribution of organisms; symbiotic
interactions; natural habitats; Nigerian biomes; soil; humans & the environment.
**Section D — Heredity & Variations**: genetics, chromosomes, heredity, variation.

## 6. Economics (id: `eco`)
Nature & scope of economics; basic economic problems; production & factors of
production; division of labour; economic systems; demand & supply; elasticity;
consumer behaviour; cost & revenue; market structures; national income;
money & banking; inflation & deflation; public finance; taxation; international
trade; balance of payments; economic development & planning; population; labour
market & unemployment.

## 7. Government (id: `gov`)
**Part I — Elements of Government**: meaning & scope of government; constitution;
political ideologies; organs/arms of government; federalism; citizenship; political
parties; pressure groups; elections & electoral systems; public opinion.
**Part II — Political Development in Nigeria**: pre-colonial polities; imperialist
penetration; decolonisation; constitutional development; post-independence
constitutions & institutions; military rule; federalism; party politics.
**Part III — Foreign Policy & Nigeria in the International Community.**
**Part IV — International Organisations** (UN, AU, ECOWAS, Commonwealth, etc.).

## 8. Literature in English (id: `lit`)
- **Drama**: types, elements, prescribed texts, African & non-African drama.
- **Prose**: types, narrative techniques, prescribed African & non-African novels.
- **Poetry**: types, elements, prescribed poems.
- **General Literary Principles**: figures of speech, literary devices, genres.
- **Literary Appreciation**: unseen passages/extracts from drama, prose, poetry.

## 9. Commerce (id: `com`)
Commerce & occupations; production; trade (home & foreign); purchase & sale of
goods; aids to trade; business units (sole trader, partnership, companies,
cooperatives); financing business; trade associations; money & banking; the stock
exchange; elements of business management; elements of marketing; legal aspects of
business (contracts, agency); information & communication technology (ICT) in
business.

## 10. Principles of Accounts (id: `acc`)
Nature & significance of bookkeeping/accounting; principles of double entry; ethics
in accounting; the cash book; bank transactions & reconciliation statements; final
accounts of a sole trader; stock valuation; control accounts & self-balancing
ledger; incomplete records & single entry; manufacturing accounts; accounts of
not-for-profit organisations; departmental & branch accounts; partnership accounts;
company accounts; public sector accounting; information technology in accounting.

---

*Question counts: 200 per subject. Sessions draw 50 questions per attempt (practice
and exam modes both capped at 50), matching the JAMB UTME structure where each
subject is 40 questions plus English at 60 in the real exam.*
